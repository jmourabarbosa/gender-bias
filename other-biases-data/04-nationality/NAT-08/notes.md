# NAT-08: Preliminary evidence of linguistic bias in academic reviewing

- **Citation:** Politzer-Ahles S., Girolamo T., Ghali S. (2020). "Preliminary evidence of linguistic bias in academic reviewing". *Journal of English for Academic Purposes* 47: 100895. DOI 10.1016/j.jeap.2020.100895. PMC7575202 (NIH author manuscript NIHMS1631807; article licensed CC BY).
- **url_used:** PMC open-data bucket https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC7575202.1/ (PMC7575202.1.pdf, xml, json, 3 figure JPGs, 4 supplementary data files). The PMC record was found through Unpaywall's oa_locations. page.html is the PolyU Scholars Hub record (first pass).
- **Access status:** downloaded (author manuscript, 22 pages)
- **Headline result found:** in the pre-registered model, abstracts with "non-standard English" received lower scientific-quality ratings than the same abstracts in "standard English" (b = -1.34, z = -1.97, p = .048). The exploratory analyses point the same way but are not significant (p = .184, .064, .209). The authors call the data "preliminary and the effects statistically inconclusive" (abstract p.1; results PDF pp.10-11).
- **Key number check ("Inconclusive (caveat)"):** MATCH. Use it as a caveat slide: one preliminary randomized study of a few abstracts, with a borderline p-value.
- **Supplementary data:** supplementary/ holds the survey export, long and wide rating tables, and the R analysis script (from PMC).
