# ETH-11: Racial inequity in grant funding from the US National Institutes of Health

- **Citation:** Taffe M. A., Gilpin N. W. (2021). "Racial inequity in grant funding from the US National Institutes of Health". *eLife* 10: e65697 (Feature article, Equity, Diversity and Inclusion). DOI 10.7554/eLife.65697.
- **url_used:** https://cdn.elifesciences.org/articles/65697/elife-65697-v2.pdf (first pass) + article.json (eLife API)
- **Access status:** downloaded (CC BY)
- **Headline number found:** white PIs had a 1.7-fold success advantage over Black PIs in FY2000-06 (29.3% vs 17.1%, Ginther 2011) and still 1.7-fold in FY2011-15 (17.7% vs 10.7%, Hoppe 2019), even though overall success fell. Box 1, PDF p.5; also abstract p.1.
- **Key number check (1.7-fold gap unchanged 2011-19):** MATCH. "2011-19" means the publication years of the two NIH studies (Ginther 2011, Hoppe 2019). The data periods are FY2000-06 and FY2011-15.
- **Note:** this is a feature/commentary article. It has no figures and no figure source data, so the "award rates side by side" slide is the redraw below. The 17.1% Black rate comes from Ginther's Table S2. ETH-08's text-derived 16.1% (29.3% minus 13.2 pp) is from the analysis sample. The small difference comes from the sample definition.
