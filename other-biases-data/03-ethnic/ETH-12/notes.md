# ETH-12: Intersectional inequalities in science

- **Citation:** Kozlowski D., Larivière V., Sugimoto C. R., Monroe-White T. (2022). "Intersectional inequalities in science". *PNAS* 119(2): e2113067119. DOI 10.1073/pnas.2113067119. PMC8764684.
- **url_used:** PMC open-data bucket https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC8764684.1/ (first pass: source.pdf, article.xml, figure JPGs, supplementary/pnas.2113067119.sapp.pdf)
- **Access status:** downloaded (CC BY-NC-ND)
- **Headline number found:** "If the author distribution over the last 40 y would have matched the 2010 US Census, there would have been 29% more articles in public health, 26% more on gender-based violence, 25% more in gynecology and in gerontology, 20% more on immigrants and minorities, and 18% more on mental health." Main text, PDF p.6 (SI Figs. S4-S6).
- **Key number check (+29% public-health papers if representative):** MATCH
- **Data/code:** GitHub https://github.com/DiegoKoz/intersectional_inequalities and interactive app https://sciencebias.uni.lu/app/ (not cloned). The underlying Web of Science data are proprietary (Clarivate).
