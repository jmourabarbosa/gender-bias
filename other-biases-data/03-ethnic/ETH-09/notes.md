# ETH-09: Topic choice contributes to the lower rate of NIH awards to African-American/Black scientists

- **Citation:** Hoppe T. A., Litovitz A., Willis K. A., Meseroll R. A., Perkins M. J., Hutchins B. I., Davis A. F., Lauer M. S., Valantine H. A., Anderson J. M., Santangelo G. M. (2019). *Science Advances* 5(10): eaaw7238. DOI 10.1126/sciadv.aaw7238. PMC6785250.
- **url_used:** PMC open-data bucket https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6785250.1/ (first pass: source.pdf, article.xml, figure JPGs, supplementary/Regression_analysis_results_for_all_variables.xlsx)
- **Access status:** downloaded (CC BY-NC)
- **Headline number found:** R01 award rate 10.7% for African-American/Black (AA/B) applicants vs 17.7% for white applicants (FY2011-2015). Text p.1 and Fig. 1, PDF p.2. Topic choice accounts for "over 20%" of the funding gap after controls (abstract p.1); "controlling for topic choice reduces the funding gap by 21%" (p.9).
- **Key number check (10.7% vs 17.7%; topic explains about 20%):** MATCH
