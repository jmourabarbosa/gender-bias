Every section also includes the counter-evidence, so the list reflects where the data are mixed. Where the publisher page could not be opened, links go to a verified mirror or press page, with the DOI linked alongside.

## Pipeline and representation

In the EU women are 48% of PhD graduates but 30% of full professors (grade A); France sits at 44% and 30%. Recent US work shows hiring, more than attrition, sets representation in most fields, though women still leave more at every rank.

## Hiring and evaluation

Identical CVs and applications are rated lower under a woman's name, and letters for women use fewer standout words. The French CNRS study (Régner 2019) shows implicit bias only hurts women's promotion in committees that deny bias exists. Williams & Ceci 2015 is the main counterpoint.

| Source | Venue | Key finding | Figure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Moss-Racusin et al. 2012](https://gap.hks.harvard.edu/science-faculty%E2%80%99s-subtle-gender-biases-favor-male-students), lab manager study ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1211286109)) | PNAS | 127 faculty rated the same application; "Jennifer" offered ~$26.5k vs. "John" ~$30.2k, and rated less competent | Competence, hireability, mentoring, salary bars |
| [Steinpreis, Anders & Ritzke 1999](https://www.engr.washington.edu/files/mycoe/faculty/docs/ImpactofGenderdoc.pdf), CV review | Sex Roles | 238 psychologists, same CV: both men and women more likely to hire the "male" applicant | Fig. 5 vote to hire by name and rater |
| [Williams & Ceci 2015](https://news.cornell.edu/node/284291), national hiring experiments ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1418878112)) | PNAS | 873 faculty preferred a woman ~2:1 over an equal man for assistant professor (hypothetical, explicitly equal shortlists) | Preference ratio by field |
| [Régner et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-019-0686-3.pdf), CNRS promotion committees ([CNRS release](https://www.cnrs.fr/en/press/even-scientists-have-gender-stereotypes-which-can-hamper-career-women-researchers)) | Nature Human Behaviour | 40 committees, 414 members: where members denied bias, stronger implicit bias meant fewer women promoted to DR; no effect where bias was acknowledged | Year 1 vs. year 2 by implicit bias × belief |

| [Madera, Hebl & Martin 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19916666/), letters for academia | J. Appl. Psychol. | 624 letters: women described as communal, less agentic; communal wording predicted worse hiring ratings | Agentic vs. communal counts |
| [Dutt et al. 2016](https://serc.carleton.edu/details/files/138789.html), geoscience postdoc letters ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1038/ngeo2819)) | Nature Geoscience | 1,224 letters: ~15% "excellent" for women vs. ~24% for men (no quality control) | Stacked letter categories |

## Funding

Raw success-rate gaps are usually small (about 1–4 points), but they grow when reviewers judge the applicant rather than the science, and award sizes differ more than award rates. Several large datasets find no gap.

## Publishing and peer review

Women hold under 30% of authorships globally and are 13% less likely to be named on articles for the same work. Large peer-review datasets mostly show little bias against manuscripts by women, but women are under-used as reviewers and editors, and lost more output during COVID.

## Pay, promotion and attrition

US women faculty earn 83.6% of men's average salary, and CNRS women earn 13.9% less on average. Parenthood is the sharpest divider: 43% of new mothers leave full-time STEM within 4–7 years, against 23% of fathers.

## Neuroscience

Women are over half of US neuroscience PhD entrants but about 30% of PhD-programme faculty and 13.8% of tenured full professors. In top neuro journals, woman–woman papers are under-cited by 13.9%, and by 23.4% in reference lists from man–man teams. Cosyne has direct data on abstract scoring and blinding.

## Interventions with evidence

The evidence on fixes is thin and some famous results are weaker than their reputation. Putting more women on committees did not help in Italy and Spain, gender-neutral tenure-clock stopping helped men more, and blinding helps only modestly. Acknowledging bias (Régner 2019) and habit-breaking training have the best support.
